# v0.4 reranker, batched (step 64)

Step 63 (`v0.4-retrieval-decision.md`) rejected every reranker on latency alone (added p95 over the
150 ms budget) and left the v0.4 holdout unread. Step 64 decodes the pairs in batches of up to 4096
tokens and 16 sequences: 40 pairs take 0.35 s instead of 4.4 s with Qwen3-Reranker-0.6B and 0.10 s
instead of 0.74 s with bge-reranker-v2-m3 (Metal), with the same scores as one pair at a time
(`batched_scores_equal_scores_one_at_a_time`). This re-measures under step 63's rule.

## Decision rule (written on 2026-10-14, before the batched build is scored)

It is not changed after the numbers are in. Agreed by the orchestrator and gpt-6-sol (high).

- The same 24 reranker runs and both no-reranker baselines as step 63, re-run on v0.2 dev with the
  batched build. Step 63's choice rule, ties, gates and conditions 1-8 apply unchanged. A tie at the
  top that step 63's ties leave open goes to the lower added p95.
- Step 63 showed the dev numbers already, so the dev choice is not blind: the v0.4 holdout is the
  confirmation that counts, and this doc says so with the result.
- Before the holdout is opened, the chosen combination's gate (1), added p95 (6) and `orag serve`
  memory (7) are checked; if one fails, the holdout stays unread.
- "Read once" means one run each of the default and the chosen combination on
  `v04-holdout-retrieval.jsonl` (paired W−L needs both), and nothing else.
- The CPU budget is still unmeasured, so a pass ships the reranker opt-in only.

## Results (2026-10-14)

**v0.2 dev, M-series Mac (Metal), batched build.** Dev W−L is unchanged from step 63 (the scores are
the same), but the latency is not better: the best run, bge-m3 + bge-reranker after promotion at
depth 30 (+8, no losses; tied with Qwen3-Reranker, broken by the lower added p95), adds **825 ms**
p95 (step 63: 629 ms); the fastest run, qwen3-embedding + bge-reranker pinned at depth 16, adds
244 ms. Condition 6 (≤ 150 ms) fails for every reranker run, so **not adopted, and the v0.4 holdout
stays unread.**

Why batching did not help: the qualification triples are one sentence each, where packing 40 pairs
into one decode is 8-12× faster; retrieval chunks are 384-512 tokens, so 16-30 pairs are 7-15k
tokens per question, and one 4096-token decode costs about as much as the pairs one by one (its
attention spans the whole batch). The batching commit is reverted; the cost is the tokens.

**CPU reference, measured once (6-core AMD EPYC 2.0 GHz VPS, AVX2, Ubuntu 24.04, release build from
CI, `--no-context-budget`, bge-m3 embedder).** Hybrid retrieval p95: **105 ms** without a reranker;
**12.6 s** with bge-reranker pinned at depth 16 (+12.5 s against the 1500 ms CPU budget). The depth
30 run was stopped after 40 minutes. The host has 6 cores, not the 8 of the reference, which can
only make a reranker look slower, but the gap is an order of magnitude.

**Reading.** On this hardware a 0.6B cross-encoder over full chunks is too slow for ORAG's offline
targets: tolerable on Apple Metal only at depth ≤ 16, and impossible on a CPU-only Windows or Linux
machine. The quality gain is real (bge-m3 + reranker: +8 on dev, no losses). Levers that remain:
rerank a short window of each chunk (the sentences around the query terms) instead of the whole
chunk, a smaller cross-encoder, or reranking only when the fused list is uncertain. Each needs its
own rule; the v0.4 holdout is still unread.
